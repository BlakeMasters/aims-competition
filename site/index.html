Exhibit A — evaluation objective

For each hidden row i, the submission returned a probability pi that the evaluated model would answer correctly. The binary outcome yi is one for a correct response and zero otherwise. The hosted score was the mean Bernoulli log-likelihood over n rows:

pᵢpredicted probability for row i
yᵢobserved binary outcome
𝒮negative mean log-likelihood shown by Codabench

AUC-ROC depends only on ordering: any monotone transformation of the probabilities preserves the ranking. Negative log-loss does not. Two submissions can produce the same ordering and different scores when their probability scales differ. This distinction determined the final method.

FOLIO 02 / HOSTED EVIDENCE

Submission construction

Each candidate was implemented as a runtime script with a small static artifact. The script received the visible subject, benchmark, condition, item content, and the labels revealed for that hosted run. It returned one clipped probability per hidden row.

  1. 01
    Specify

    Define a probability estimator, its calibration parameters, and the evidence it may use at runtime.

  2. 02
    Compile

    Estimate subject and domain statistics from public data, then serialize only the tables required by the runtime.

  3. 03
    Validate

    Package the script and artifact, verify the submission contract, and run a local smoke test.

  4. 04
    Evaluate

    Submit the package, record the hosted score and status, and use that evidence to select the next hypothesis.

The cycle moved from global centers and subject priors to text features, ranking ensembles, compact domain priors, and finally revealed-label anchoring. Submission scripts were the experimental unit: each package fixed a method, its parameters, and its runtime evidence boundary.

FOLIO 03 / CALIBRATION

Calibration under fixed ranking

Two calibration operations recur throughout the submission search. Shrinkage controls the variance of estimates supported by few observations. Logit rescaling controls the confidence of an ordered set of predictions.

The sandbox uses six predictions with fixed ordering. Adjusting β isolates the effect of probability scale: AUC-ROC remains constant while log-loss changes.

The ordering is unchanged; the probability estimates are not.

FOLIO 04 / FINAL METHOD

Domain priors and revealed-label anchoring

The hidden benchmarks were absent from public training data. Exact item lookup and benchmark-specific effects could therefore fail under distribution shift. The stable fallback used coarse item domains and subject ability as priors rather than as direct hidden-benchmark identifiers.

The first-place family used random revealed labels to estimate the base rate of the hidden run. It then refined that estimate through a hierarchy of visible groupings and applied a bounded family-relative subject correction.

ℓ̃g*shrunk hidden-run base-rate logit
ōLmean subject offset in the revealed sample
os,ftarget subject’s family-relative offset
βbounded correction strength

The runtime applied the anchored estimate whenever revealed-label support was available and otherwise returned the compact domain prior. This separation provided a stable no-label baseline while allowing the hidden-run evidence to determine the final probability center.
